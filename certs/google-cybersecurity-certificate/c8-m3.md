## Understanding Stakeholders

**Stakeholder** – An individual or group that has an interest in any decision or activity of an organization.

### Levels of Stakeholders

| Role | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Cybersecurity Risk Manager** | Leads efforts to identify, assess, and mitigate security risks. |
| **CEO (Chief Executive Officer)** | Highest-ranking person in the organization. |
| **CFO (Chief Financial Officer)** | Focuses on financial impact of security incidents. |
| **CISO (Chief Information Security Officer)** | Highest level of security stakeholder. |
| **Operations Manager** | Oversees day-to-day security operations and implementation. |

### Communication Flow
- Entry-level analysts communicate most with operations managers and risk managers.
- These stakeholders report up to CISOs and CFOs.
- Your reports eventually reach top-level stakeholders through your team.

## Communicating Effectively with Stakeholders

### Key Questions to Ask Yourself
- What do I want this person to know?
- Why is it important for them to know it?
- When do they need to take action?
- How do I explain the situation in a nontechnical manner?

### Follow Protocols
Learn acceptable communication methods:
- In-person meetings
- Video conferencing
- Emails
- Company chat applications

### Tailor Your Message
- **Operations managers** – Focus on day-to-day operations (e.g., logging anomalies).
- **Senior stakeholders** – Focus on risks and financial impact (e.g., potential costs of breaches).

### Communication Methods
| Method | Best For |
|--------|----------|
| Instant messaging | Straightforward updates |
| Email | Complex situations with multiple layers |
| Phone calls | Quick communication |
| Video calling | Remote meetings |
| Spreadsheets | Data-heavy information |
| Slideshows | Presentations |

## Visual Communication Using Dashboards

**Visual dashboard** – A way of displaying various types of data quickly in one place.

### When to Use Visuals
- Communicating numbers and data
- Showing trends over time
- Comparing multiple departments or categories
- Making complex data easy to understand

### Dashboard Types
- **Simple** – Single chart or graph
- **Complex** – Multiple charts, graphs, and tables

### Tools for Creating Dashboards
- Google Sheets
- Apache OpenOffice
- Other data visualization software

### When to Use vs. Not Use Visuals
- **Use visuals** – Presenting audit results, comparing departments, showing trends
- **Use other methods** – Simple updates, one-on-one discussions, quick notifications

## Juliana's Story: Effective Communication

### Scenario Summary
Juliana communicated two security incidents to stakeholders:

**Incident #1 – Account Lockouts**
- Data showing number of locked accounts across departments
- **Communication method:** Visual dashboard (charts and graphs)
- **Audience:** Senior executives of five departments

**Incident #2 – Near Data Breach**
- Detailed report on what happened, what worked, and what needs improvement
- **Communication method:** Detailed document with timelines
- **Audience:** CISO (via her manager)

### Key Takeaway
Match your communication method to what matters most to the stakeholder:
- Data/numbers → Visual dashboard
- Processes/procedures → Detailed document with timelines
