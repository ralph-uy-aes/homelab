## Incident Escalation

**Incident escalation** – The process of identifying a potential security incident, triaging it, and handing it off to a more experienced team member.

### Why Escalation Matters
- **Breach notification laws** – Many countries require notification of breaches involving PII.
- **Low-level issues can escalate** – Even small incidents (e.g., multiple failed logins, unapproved software downloads) need investigation and possible escalation.

### Escalation Process
Every organization has unique escalation policies detailing:
- Who should be notified when an alert is received
- Who to contact if first responder is unavailable
- How to escalate (IT desk, incident management tool, direct communication)

### Low-Level Security Issues
- Employee has one failed login attempt
- Employee downloads unapproved software

These are not significant security challenges but must be investigated in case they need escalation.

## Roles and Responsibilities During Escalation

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|----------------|
| **Data Owner** | Decides who can access, edit, use, or destroy information. Administrative control over specific data. |
| **Data Controller** | Determines procedure and purpose for processing data. Ensures compliance with privacy regulations. |
| **Data Processor** | Processes data on behalf of the data controller. Typically a vendor; implements security measures. |
| **Data Custodian** | Assigns/removes access, implements security controls, grants/revokes access, monitors data. |
| **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** | Monitors internal compliance with data protection procedures; advises on obligations; conducts assessments. |

## Escalation Timing and Decision-Making

### Key Principles
- **Your decisions matter** – Analysts must make daily decisions to keep data and systems safe.
- **Trust your instincts** – Confidence is important; ask questions when needed.
- **All security events are not equal** – Recognize which assets are most important to prioritize incidents.

### Incident Classification Types
| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| **Malware Infection** | Malicious software infiltrates organization's computers or network. |
| **Unauthorized Access** | Individual gains digital/physical access without permission. |
| **Improper Usage** | Employee violates acceptable use policies. |

### Prioritization
- Incidents impacting **essential business operations** take priority.
- Example: Unauthorized access to a manufacturing application > malware on a non-critical legacy system.

### Quick Escalation Tips
1. Familiarize yourself with the escalation policy
2. Follow the policy at all times
3. Ask questions when uncertain

## Module 2 Glossary

- **Data controller** – Determines procedure and purpose for processing data
- **Data processor** – Processes data on behalf of the data controller
- **Data Protection Officer (DPO)** – Monitors compliance of data protection procedures
- **Escalation policy** – Outlines who to notify and how incidents should be handled
- **Improper usage** – Violation of acceptable use policies
- **Incident escalation** – Identifying, triaging, and handing off incidents to more experienced team members

- **Unauthorized access** – Gaining digital/physical access without permission
